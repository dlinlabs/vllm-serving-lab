# Three-round debate and evidence-based adjudication

Four native pr32 Polecats independently addressed the same full design problem:
obsidian/Claude Opus5.5, quartz/GPT-6 Astra, jasper/Gemini3.8 Flash and
onyx/GPT-6.1 Sol. Their complete per-round artifacts are preserved in proposals/.
The execution ledger records actual usage models, native branches, event logs
and artifact hashes. Profiles alone are not the verification basis.

Round1 conclusions were not shared until all four independent reports existed.
Round2 read all peers, supplied counterexamples and challenged mathematical,
physical, computational and experimental claims. Round3 revised the proposals
and reviewed the Mayor's concrete draft. Earlier rounds are kept, including
incorrect statements, rather than rewritten to fabricate agreement.

The resulting ADR selects a default-off bounded gateway-admission challenger,
not a scientifically established winning MPC. The lower-score finite-tail rule
is explicit and can be biased; tail sensitivity is mandatory. A richer
kernel-inspired model is not selected merely because it looks physical.

Substantive retractions include Opus's ceiling/mean-release/terminal/greedy/
rule-of-three claims and Gemini's absent-KV chance/safe-C24/unmeasured runtime/
native-acceptance claims. The original reports remain evidence of the debate;
their unsupported statements are not incorporated as findings. No "unanimous"
phrase or numerical vote is used to establish a physical or statistical fact.

Still unresolved: fixed fallback16 versus32 on the current host; natural-EOS
residence sufficiency; actual prefill/decode interference; prediction/control
horizon; cadence and compute budget; coherent live observation latency;
counterfactual uncertainty; cancellation/orphan work; whether feedback or MPC
wins. The identification and benchmark protocols specify decisive experiments.
Absent native evidence, these remain open rather than settled by consensus.
